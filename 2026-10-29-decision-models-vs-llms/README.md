# Decision models vs. LLMs

**Started** October 29, 2026 · **Status** Done · [Write-up](https://usmankhan.io/lab/decision-models-vs-llms/)

Is a decision model better than the LLM it was built from, or just an LLM forced to answer in one token?

## The question

TypeSafe released Jev in September 2026: send it text plus a typed question (yes/no, pick one, score 1–5) and it returns a probability per option instead of writing a reply. Within six weeks OpenRouter had a dozen compatible "decision models" from Perplexity, Microsoft, Baseten, Liquid and others.

Every public test compared Jev to a frontier model 10–50x its size: it ties on read-it-off-the-page questions and loses by ~10 points on reasoning. That measures size, not the idea. The question nobody had answered is whether the decision head does anything, or whether it's a normal LLM with a different billing model.

Two of the clones state their base. Baseten Elephant 1 and Microsoft-Decision-1 are both post-trained from Qwen3.5-9B. So raw Qwen3.5-9B, forced to one token with logprobs, can be run against the two decision models built on it, on identical questions. Same weights, different head.


## What I expected

Written before running: didn't know. Public tests made Jev look like a cheap fine-tune (good on lookups, bad on reasoning, flips on label renames). If the 9B heads were no better than raw Qwen3.5-9B, "decision model" is a pricing trick. If better, the category is real and the question becomes which one.

## Method

Full brief in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md); how to run it in [RUNNING.md](RUNNING.md).

1. **Arms.** Decision: Jev 1.14, Perplexity Decider V1.2 (27B), Baseten Elephant 1 (Qwen3.5-9B), Microsoft-Decision-1 (Qwen3.5-9B), Liquid d1-ultra. Instant LLM (`max_tokens=1`, logprobs, temperature 0): raw Qwen3.5-9B, raw Qwen3.5-27B. Reasoning ceiling: GPT-6.1 Sol. Dumb baseline: regex on tier 1, majority class on tier 2. A cheap closed LLM arm (GPT-5 Nano / Haiku 4.5) was dropped: neither returns logprobs through OpenRouter. Follow-up ([HANDOFF-2.md](HANDOFF-2.md)): raw Qwen3.5-9B with thinking on (same provider, 12k-token cap, then one token), and the one-token control under a terse and a careful system prompt on tier 2.
2. **Questions.** 450, all generated by code with ground truth by construction. Tier 1 (200): answer is in the text (tickets, invoices, memos, logs, contracts, with distractors). Tier 2 (200): answer needs reasoning over the text (room scheduling, expense policy, event order, zebra puzzles; solver + independent checker). Tier 3 (50): outside knowledge, generated by Claude Fable 5.1 and checked by GPT-6 Astra; a confound demo, not a real tier. Mix per tier ~40/40/20 yes-no / choice / score. Plus a 50-item label-swap subset run as yes/no, 1/0 and A/B.
3. **Runs.** 5 runs per question per model, concurrency 4 per model from one machine, every call checkpointed to SQLite with its raw JSON. 24,000 calls, 27 minutes, $3.83 (of which $3.25 was GPT). Follow-up: 5,000 calls over five hours, $2.00 (of which $1.89 was the thinking arm). $5.83 in all.
4. **Measures.** Accuracy and macro-F1, Brier and ECE, flip rate across the 5 runs, invalid-output rate, label-swap sensitivity and AUC, latency p50/p95, cost per 1k from OpenRouter's usage records, matched-pair deltas with bootstrap 95% CIs, routing simulation (decision model answers above confidence τ, GPT below).

## Results

**The decision head is a format fine-tune, and Jev isn't the good one.**

- Forcing Qwen3.5-9B to one token costs it 46 points on tier 2: 0.992 with thinking on, 0.531 without. The two heads built on it give back 15 and 10 of those points. Elephant 1 0.685 (+0.154 over the one-token base, CI [0.085, 0.220]; -0.307 against the thinking base, CI [-0.371, -0.248]); Microsoft-Decision-1 0.628 (+0.097, CI [0.027, 0.169]; -0.364, CI [-0.425, -0.298]). They do it at about 1/50th the cost and 1/60th to 1/90th the latency of thinking (on these providers): $0.016 and $0.014 per 1k vs $0.77, 261 and 368 ms p50 vs 23.5 s. Thinking Qwen3.5-9B beats every decision model on tier 2, Decider (0.840) included. Brier improves with the head the same way (Elephant 0.43 vs 0.60). Decider is 0.840 vs raw Qwen3.5-27B 0.525 (+0.315, CI [0.240, 0.390]), at 1/12th the cost.
- Tier 1 is solved: every model ≥0.94, two at 1.000, baseline 0.54. Can't separate models.
- Tier 2 order: GPT-6.1 Sol 1.000 ($1.49/1k, 2.4 s) · Decider 0.840 ($0.009) · d1-ultra 0.729 · Elephant 0.685 · Microsoft-Decision-1 0.628 · Jev 0.606 ($0.030) · Qwen3.5-9B 0.531 · Qwen3.5-27B 0.525 · baseline 0.36.
- Jev is the least consistent decision model once you count both flips and labels: 12.5% of tier-2 questions flipped across 5 runs (d1-ultra 13.5%, but near-zero on labels), 8.8% of yes/no answers moved on label rename. Decider and Elephant: 0 and 0. Raw Qwen3.5-9B was the worst on labels (13.6%), so the decision training fixes label sensitivity; Jev got less of it.
- [Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26758)'s label-swap collapse (AUC 0.81 → 0.58) did not replicate: Jev went 0.945 → 0.940 here.
- Routing, tier 2: Decider at τ=0.96 routes 42% to GPT, 0.98 accuracy, $0.68/1k vs $1.49 for GPT alone. Jev needs 60% routed to reach 0.98, at $0.97.
- Tier 3: Jev 0.98, other decision models 0.92–0.94; CIs overlap at n=50 and GPT's 1.00 is circular. Only hint is that Jev is probably the largest base.

Caveats: Decider's base is undisclosed (27B from its listing); Alibaba caps Qwen 27B logprobs at top 5 and has no logit_bias, but its invalid-output rate was 0 so the 0.525 is the model's; latency isn't comparable across providers (27B tier-2 p95 7.9 s); GPT's calibration is verbalized; GPT scoring 1.000 on tier 2 means the routing savings are an upper bound; no decision model exists on a base above 27B.

All tables are in [results/](results/) (`metrics_by_tier.csv`, `matched_pair_deltas.csv`, `labelswap*.csv`, `routing.csv`, `REPORT.md`). Questions in [data/questions.jsonl](data/questions.jsonl). The per-call raw JSON (~48k files) and `runs.sqlite` (180 MB) are not in git; ask if you want them.

### Follow-up (2026-10-30)

Two controls run before publishing ([HANDOFF-2.md](HANDOFF-2.md); run `followup-01`, 5,000 calls, $2.00, analysed together with `main-01`). The thinking-Qwen result is folded into the first bullet above. The second control:

- **The prompt moves the one-token control by 3 points overall, 15 on score questions.** Tier 2 under three system prompts: 0.531 (original), 0.497 (terse), 0.530 (careful). Range 3.4 points, CI [1.0, 9.1]. By format: yes/no 6.5, choice 4.7, score 14.5 points (40 questions). Invalid rate 0 under all three. The 0.531 is the model, not the prompt; the 1–5 score questions are the exception.
- Thinking at temperature 0 looped to the 12k cap with no answer on 86 of 2,250 base calls (tier 1 4.8%, tier 2 0.7%, tier 3 12.4%); those count as wrong. The reasoning arm's label-swap sensitivity (0.144 in `labelswap_sensitivity.csv`) is inflated by the same cap-outs, so it is left out of the comparisons.

## What's next

- Add Baseten Mammoth 1 (stated Qwen3.5-27B) as a clean 27B pair.
- Nobody has a decision head on a base above 27B; finding out whether it helps at 70B means training one (Amazon's Strands Decider and Baseten publish their recipes).
- If picking one for routing/guardrails today and it has to answer in under a second: Decider, with a threshold calibrated on a few hundred of your own labelled examples. If latency isn't the constraint, a thinking Qwen3.5-9B at $0.77/1k (0.992 on tier 2) is a better fallback target than GPT at $1.49 (1.000).
